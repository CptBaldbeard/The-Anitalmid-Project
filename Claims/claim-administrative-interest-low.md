# anitalmid-birkman-5: Low Administrative interest disqualifies clerical IT roles

**Source:** [[Dan Bechtel Birkman Profile]] — Birkman Basics Report (G6HVDQ, 2025-09-05)

## The Claim

Dan's Administrative interest score of 20% is a strong negative signal for roles where the primary work is process administration, data entry, routine paperwork, or bureaucratic compliance management. This includes: pure helpdesk ticket triage (without technical depth), IT asset management clerks, change management administrators (CAB-only roles without technical implementation), and compliance auditors who review but don't build.

The claim is NOT that Dan cannot perform administrative tasks — his career history shows he can (access reviews, documentation maintenance, CJIS compliance). The claim is that Administrative-heavy roles will drain him because they misalign with his intrinsic interests.

## Evidence

- Administrative interest: 20% (9th of 10 areas, only above Numerical at 17%)
- Technical interest: 82% (62-point differential)
- Scientific interest: 92% (72-point differential)
- Strengths emphasize investigation, creativity, hands-on problem-solving — not process administration
- Stress Behavior ■ in Yellow: under stress, "become over-insistent on rules" — excessive administrative burden could trigger this stress response

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Quantitative score with large differential against aligned interests. The stress behavior pattern provides a mechanism: administrative overload triggers rigidity.

## Stakes

This claim is a gatekeeper. It filters OUT entire categories of IT roles (clerical, pure administration, CAB-only positions) and channels career recommendations toward builder/analyst/architect roles where administrative work is incidental, not primary.

## Edges

**Supports:**
- [[claim-systems-administrator-primary-fit]] — Systems administration involves administration of SYSTEMS (building, configuring, troubleshooting), not administration of PROCESSES
- [[claim-security-analyst-fit]] — Security analysis is investigative, not administrative

**Contradicts:**
- [[claim-compliance-documentation-experience]] — Note: this is an APPARENT contradiction but not a real one. Compliance DOCUMENTATION (writing about controls) aligns with Literary interest. Compliance ADMINISTRATION (managing audit schedules, chasing evidence) would not. The distinction is important.

**Challenged by:**
- [[claim-structured-thinking-strength]] — Structured thinking could be misinterpreted as administrative aptitude; the Birkman data distinguishes them clearly

## Assessment

The 20% Administrative interest is one of the most important NEGATIVE signals in the profile. Combined with the 82% Technical interest, the message is clear: Dan wants to work ON systems, not administer processes ABOUT systems. Career recommendations that ignore this signal will produce role suggestions that look plausible on paper ("he's organized!") but misalign with intrinsic motivation.
