# anitalmid-strength-1: Learning agility, open-source advocacy, and collaborative mindset

**Source:** [[Resume/Dan Bechtel Resume]] — Additional Strengths section

## The Claim

Dan's resume identifies three behavioral strengths with direct career relevance:

1. **Learning Agility** — The ability to rapidly acquire new technical skills and adapt to changing technologies. Corroborated by career history: Dan has moved across 7 roles spanning technical support, telecommunications, video production, systems administration, security operations, and GRC documentation — demonstrating repeated successful domain transitions.

2. **Open-Source Advocate** — A value alignment with transparency, community-driven development, and security-through-visibility. The professional summary explicitly states "Passionate about open-source security and building secure systems that protect organizations and users." This aligns with the Linux hardened repository work at City of Moscow and the broader IT industry shift toward open-source tooling.

3. **Collaborative Mindset** — Ability to work effectively across teams and disciplines. Corroborated by cross-functional collaborations: engineering + business teams at Valdyr.io, university library + student union at University of Idaho, and 200+ distributed employees at City of Moscow.

## Evidence

### Learning Agility:
- 7 distinct professional roles with successful transitions between domains
- Security+ certification earned while working full-time
- Skill progression from help desk → systems administration → security operations
- Resume lists "Learning Agility" explicitly as an additional strength

### Open-Source Advocacy:
- Professional summary: "Passionate about open-source security"
- Linux hardened repository engineering (open-source stack)
- Aligns with Birkman Blue Interests: "create new approaches, think of new approaches, work with ideas"

### Collaborative Mindset:
- Cross-functional work at Valdyr.io (engineering + business)
- University Library and Student Union deployment (multi-stakeholder)
- 200+ distributed employee support (diverse user base)
- Aligns with Birkman strength: "You appreciate an environment where everyone wins together"

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium

**Rationale:** Self-reported strengths from resume. Learning agility and collaborative mindset have corroborating career-history patterns. Open-source advocacy is stated in the professional summary but lacks a public portfolio of contributions (e.g., GitHub activity, project maintainership). Upgrade to high if open-source contributions are documented.

## Stakes

These three strengths are career accelerators. Learning agility is the single most important trait for IT career longevity — technology changes, and the ability to adapt is more valuable than any specific skill. Open-source advocacy aligns with industry values and suggests Dan would thrive in modern DevOps and cloud-native environments where open-source tooling dominates. Collaborative mindset is essential for progression beyond individual contributor roles.

## Assessment

These strengths are soft factors that distinguish Dan from equally-credentialed candidates. In a field where many professionals have Security+ and sysadmin experience, learning agility and open-source advocacy signal career trajectory — the difference between someone who maintains systems and someone who evolves them. The collaborative mindset addresses a stereotype risk: the introverted, isolated sysadmin. Dan's Birkman profile shows introversion (Blue/Yellow) but his career history shows effective cross-functional collaboration — introversion ≠ inability to collaborate.
