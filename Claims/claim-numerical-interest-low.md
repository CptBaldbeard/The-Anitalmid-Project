# anitalmid-birkman-6: Low Numerical interest suggests avoiding pure quantitative IT roles

**Source:** [[Dan Bechtel Birkman Profile]] — Birkman Basics Report (G6HVDQ, 2025-09-05)

## The Claim

Dan's Numerical interest at 17% (lowest of all 10 Birkman interest areas) signals low intrinsic attraction to pure mathematical, statistical, or computational work. This does NOT disqualify him from IT generally — most IT roles (systems administration, cloud engineering, security operations) require logic and structured thinking but not advanced mathematics. It DOES suggest avoiding: data science, machine learning engineering, quantitative analysis, actuarial IT, financial systems modeling, and cryptography engineering.

## Evidence

- Numerical interest: 17% (lowest of 10)
- Gap analysis: 75 points below Scientific (92%) — clear preference for qualitative/systematic analysis over quantitative/mathematical
- Gap analysis: 80 points below Musical (97%) — profile extremes are informative
- Strength #8: "structured and organized in your thinking and approach" — structured thinking, not numerical thinking

## Confidence

**Rating:** high

**Rationale:** Quantitative score is unambiguous. However, many successful IT professionals have low Numerical interest — IT is more about systems thinking than mathematics. The confidence is moderated by the limited applicability of Numerical interest to most IT roles.

## Stakes

Lower stakes than the Administrative interest claim. Numerical interest primarily gates data science and quantitative roles, which were never primary candidates given the rest of the profile. The claim's main value is preventing misdirection toward "analytics" roles that sound technical but are actually mathematical.

## Edges

**Depends on:**
- [[claim-scientific-interest-92-percent]] — Scientific interest WITHOUT Numerical interest is the key pattern: analytical but not mathematical

**Supports:**
- [[claim-systems-administrator-primary-fit]] — Systems administration is systems thinking, not mathematical thinking

**Contradicts:**
- None directly — no major IT career path is contradicted by low Numerical interest

## Assessment

17% Numerical interest is a soft constraint, not a hard gate. It rules out data science and quantitative analysis but leaves all major IT career paths (systems, security, cloud, networking, GRC, technical writing) fully viable. The claim is useful for precision — it prevents the recommendation engine from drifting toward "analytics" because "he's analytical."
