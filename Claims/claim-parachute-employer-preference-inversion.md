# anitalmid-parachute-5: Employer preference inversion during hard times creates a structural job-search mismatch

**Source:** [[Parachute/What Color Is Your Parachute Framework]] — Chapter 5, 2012 edition

## The Claim

Bolles identifies a "profoundly overlooked fact": employers and job-hunters typically search for each other in EXACTLY OPPOSITE ways. During good times, employers adapt to job-hunter preferences (posting vacancies online, reading resumes). During hard times, employers revert to their own preferences — internal candidates, proof of competency (portfolios), and trusted referrals — while job-hunters continue using job boards and resumes. This structural mismatch, not a lack of vacancies, explains why job-hunting methods that worked during expansion fail during contraction.

## Evidence

### The Preference Inversion Diagram

Bolles presents (in Chapter 5 of the 2012 edition) a ranked list of how employers prefer to fill vacancies versus how job-hunters prefer to search:

| Employer's Preferred Methods (During Hard Times) | Job-Hunter's Preferred Methods |
|---|---|
| 1. Internal hire / promotion from within | 1. Internet job boards (Monster, CareerBuilder, Indeed) |
| 2. Proof of competency (portfolio, GitHub, work samples) | 2. Sending out resumes |
| 3. Hire through trusted colleague referral | 3. LinkedIn/Facebook/Twitter networking |
| 4. Hire through recruiter/headhunter | 4. Job-hunter support groups |
| 5. Job posting on company's own website | 5. Newspaper classifieds (declining) |
| 6. Job posting on external job boards | 6. Cold calling / walking in |

### Supporting Data

- 3,907,000 people found jobs in the month Bolles was writing (2011-2012)
- 3,093,000 vacancies remained unfilled the same month
- His conclusion: "Can't find" ≠ "don't exist"

### The Car Key Analogy

Bolles uses the analogy of losing car keys: "You can't find them" does not mean "they don't exist." The same applies to jobs during downturns. The gap is in the SEARCH METHOD, not the vacancy count.

### Why This Happens — The Economics

During good times: employers struggle to fill vacancies → they accommodate job-hunters' preferred channels (posting on job boards, reviewing resumes).

During hard times: employers find it easy to fill vacancies → they revert to their default preferences (internal candidates, trusted referrals, proof of work).

The job-hunter who only knows how to search during good times is structurally unprepared for hard times. Bolles calls this "absolute hell" — and it's why unemployment can drag on for 27+ months.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** Direct extraction from Chapter 5's explicit argument. Bolles presents the preference inversion as a documented pattern from his 40-year counseling practice and supports it with BLS employment statistics. The claim does not depend on interpretation — it is Bolles' stated thesis.

## Stakes

This is arguably the single most actionable insight in the entire book for Dan's current situation. It means:

1. **Dan should STOP using job boards as his primary search method.** They work during good times because employers adapt to them. During the current market, employers have reverted to internal candidates + referrals.

2. **Dan should LEAN INTO his Birkman strengths.** His strongest job-search channels (one-on-one informational interviewing, written portfolio/documentation, GitHub open-source contributions) map to the employer's preferred methods (proof of competency, trusted referrals).

3. **Every resume sent into a black hole is evidence of the inversion, not personal failure.** The structural mismatch operates regardless of resume quality.

## Assessment

The employer preference inversion is the Rosetta Stone for understanding why the Parachute methodology works. Bolles doesn't just say "network more" — he explains WHY networking works (it accesses the employer's preferred hiring channel) and WHY job boards don't (they access the employer's least-preferred channel, used only during labor shortages). For Dan specifically, this insight transforms his job search from "try harder at what's not working" to "use the channels your personality is already optimized for." The D-T-P skills grid (parachute-1) identifies WHAT to offer; the preference inversion identifies HOW to offer it.
