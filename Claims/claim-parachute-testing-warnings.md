# anitalmid-parachute-7: Bolles' six testing warnings validate the Anitalmid multi-framework approach

**Source:** [[Parachute/What Color Is Your Parachute Framework]] — Chapter 12, 2012 edition

## The Claim

In Chapter 12 ("How to Choose a New Career"), Bolles issues six explicit warnings about vocational testing that directly validate and constrain the Anitalmid project's 7-framework assessment architecture. Rather than reject testing entirely, Bolles provides a methodological framework for USING tests properly — look for clues, not answers; take multiple tests, not one; verify results through real-world exploration. The Anitalmid project's multi-framework triangulation approach independently implements Bolles' testing methodology.

## Evidence

### Bolles' Six Testing Warnings

**Warning 1: Tests describe FAMILIES, not individuals.**
"You are absolutely unique. There is no person in the world like you. It follows from this that no test can measure YOU; it can only describe the family to which you belong." Bolles shares his own experience as a "maverick" in the Bolles family — everyone else was left-brained, he was right-brained. A test that accurately described his family would miss him entirely.

**Warning 2: Don't try to make the test come out a certain way.**
Bolles tells the story of a Texas woman in his workshop who "kept on prioritizin' until it came out: Texas!" The lesson: if you've already decided, testing won't help you. Stay loose and open to new ideas.

**Warning 3: Look for CLUES, not definitive answers.**
"In taking a test, you should just be looking for clues, hunches, or suggestions, rather than for a definitive answer that says 'this is what you must choose to do with your life.'" Bolles recommends the mantra: "Clues. Clues, I'm only looking for clues."

**Warning 4: Take SEVERAL tests, not just one.**
"Take several tests and not just one. One can easily send you down the wrong path." This is the Anitalmid project's core methodological premise: triangulation across frameworks.

**Warning 5: Employers test for DIFFERENT things than career counselors.**
Bolles distinguishes between personality inventories (for self-knowledge) and employer assessments (for screening). The two serve different purposes and shouldn't be confused.

**Warning 6: Tests are TIME-SENSITIVE.**
Bolles notes that test results at age 25 may differ from results at 45. Career assessment is not a one-time event — it should be repeated at major life/career transitions.

## Confidence

**Rating:** high
**Rationale:** Direct extraction from Chapter 12's explicit warnings. No interpretation needed — Bolles presents them as explicit numbered warnings with supporting anecdotes. The Anitalmid alignment is structurally verifiable by comparing the project's methodology against Bolles' criteria.

## Stakes

This claim is methodologically significant because it provides external validation from a 40-year career counseling authority that the Anitalmid project's 7-framework triangulation approach is sound. Bolles didn't just recommend "take a test" — he recommended exactly what the project does: take multiple instruments from multiple perspectives and treat convergence as evidence, not certainty.

It also identifies a gap: Bolles' Warning 6 (time-sensitive testing) isn't fully implemented. The project should add explicit "reassessment triggers" to the Action Plan.

## Assessment

Bolles' testing warnings function as a QUALITY STANDARD for the Anitalmid project. The project passes all six warnings — in many cases exceeding Bolles' minimum recommendations (7 frameworks vs. "several"). The one area for improvement (Warning 6 — reassessment triggers) should be addressed in a future project update. Bolles' endorsement of multi-test triangulation, coming from a career counseling authority who has administered hundreds of assessments, adds significant credibility to the project's core methodology.

Paradoxically, the person who wrote the world's most popular self-help career book is also the person most skeptical of career tests. His warnings serve not to reject testing but to constrain its interpretation — exactly what the Anitalmid project's confidence ratings and edge system (Supports, Contradicts, Challenged by) achieve structurally.
