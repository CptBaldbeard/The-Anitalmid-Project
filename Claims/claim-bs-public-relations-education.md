# anitalmid-edu-1: BS in Public Relations from University of Idaho

**Source:** [[Resume/Dan Bechtel Resume]] — Education section

## The Claim

Dan's formal education is in Public Relations — a communications discipline focused on strategic messaging, audience analysis, and translating complex information for diverse stakeholders. This degree amplifies his Birkman Literary interest (92%) and written-word strength into a trained, professional competency. It explains why Dan's documentation (ISO 27001, SOC2, CJIS, KB articles, GRC SaaS platform content) is both technically accurate AND accessible to non-technical audiences.

## Evidence

- Bachelor of Science in Public Relations, University of Idaho (listed on resume)
- Corroborated by career output:
  - Technical Writer at Valdyr.io: translated ISO 27001/SOC2 requirements for diverse audiences
  - Information Systems Specialist: maintained CJIS security documentation supporting audit requirements
  - Vandal Card Specialist: standardized IT service procedures and developed knowledge base
  - Technology Solutions Partner: designed and implemented support systems with documentation
- Corroborated by Birkman:
  - Literary interest: 92% (top 3 of 10 areas)
  - Written word strength: "You like working with the written word, which may involve anything from documentation to the production of marketing materials"

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Verifiable university degree. Directly corroborated by Birkman Literary interest score (92%) and written-word strength statement, plus 3+ career roles involving technical documentation output.

## Stakes

This claim resolves a previous ambiguity in the graph: was Dan's documentation skill purely a Birkman aptitude signal, or is it a trained, professional competency? The answer is both. The Public Relations degree provides formal training in audience analysis and message adaptation — skills that make Dan's documentation more effective than a purely technical writer's would be. This differentiates him from the majority of IT professionals and strengthens the Documentation Engineering assessment in the Capstone.

## Assessment

A Public Relations degree is an unconventional educational background for an IT professional — and that is precisely its value. It makes Dan a "bilingual" professional: fluent in both technical systems AND audience-appropriate communication. This is a rare and valuable combination in IT, where poor documentation and miscommunication are persistent industry problems. The degree, combined with Birkman Literary interest (92%), suggests documentation and stakeholder communication will remain differentiators throughout Dan's career regardless of technical specialization.
