# anitalmid-birkman-9: Investigating/troubleshooting strength supports security and operations roles

**Source:** [[Dan Bechtel Birkman Profile]] — Birkman Basics Report (G6HVDQ, 2025-09-05)

## The Claim

Dan's Strength #1 — "You like investigating situations — often in a research or trouble-shooting capacity" — is the single most career-relevant strength for IT operations and security roles. Security analysts investigate alerts and incidents. Systems administrators troubleshoot infrastructure failures. Cloud engineers diagnose performance issues. The investigative drive is the behavioral engine that sustains these roles through the mundane (false positives) and the critical (active incidents).

## Evidence

### Birkman direct:
- Strength #1: "You like investigating situations — often in a research or trouble-shooting capacity"
- Scientific interest: 92% — intrinsic attraction to systematic inquiry
- Strength #11: "can handle ambiguous situations well, where the best answer may be less obvious" — ambiguity tolerance under investigation

### Career history corroboration:
- Information Systems Specialist: "Monitored and responded to security alerts and suspicious activity, investigating incidents as first-level responder"
- Information Systems Specialist: "Provided first-touch support for security and IT service desk tickets for 200+ distributed employees"
- AppleCare iOS Chat Advisor: "Resolved iOS software and hardware issues" — troubleshooting at scale
- Vandal Card Specialist: "Standardized IT service procedures and developed knowledge base reducing average Help Desk ticket resolution time" — institutionalized troubleshooting

## Confidence

**Rating:** very-high

**Rationale:** Explicit strength statement + three corroborating Birkman data points + four career history examples spanning 2015-2025. Ten-year pattern of investigative behavior.

## Stakes

This claim gates whether Dan should pursue operations/security (investigative) vs. pure development (constructive) IT roles. The evidence strongly favors operations/security. A developer writes code to create new functionality; an operator investigates why existing functionality broke. Dan's profile and history point to the latter.

The ten-year pattern of investigative behavior across four different roles (AppleCare, Vandal Card, Technology Solutions Partner, Information Systems Specialist) shows this is not situational — it is dispositional.
