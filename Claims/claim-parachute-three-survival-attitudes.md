# anitalmid-parachute-6: Three survival attitudes map to Dan's personality architecture

**Source:** [[Parachute/What Color Is Your Parachute Framework]] — Chapter 3, 2012 edition

## The Claim

Bolles identifies three attitudes as the emotional foundation for job-hunting survival, and positions them BEFORE any techniques in his five-part framework. These three attitudes — persistent tenacity (keeping at least two alternatives alive at all times), active adaptability (reframing identity beyond job-title), and deliberate optimism (continuing to learn, create, and grow during unemployment) — form a resilience architecture that maps to Dan's Birkman Needs (systematic environment, concentrated work time) and Enneagram Type 4's core drive for meaning through difficulty.

## Evidence

### The Three Attitudes (Chapter 3)

**Attitude 1: Persistent Tenacity — "Always have at least two alternatives"**

Bolles' central finding on Hope: "Hope requires that, in every situation, we have at least two alternatives." He cites a study of 100 job-hunters: those using only ONE method had a 51% dropout rate by month 2; those using TWO OR MORE had only a 31% dropout rate.

The "at least two" principle applies to: self-description (job-title AND skills-based), search methods, target roles, target company sizes, target locations. This is the Hope Mechanism.

**Attitude 2: Active Adaptability — Redefine yourself beyond your old job-title**

Bolles argues that many unemployed people cling to their old job-title as their sole identity, which limits the range of jobs they can apply for. He advocates describing yourself as "a person who has THESE skills, and THESE knowledges, and THIS experience" — exactly the approach the Anitalmid project takes with claims extraction and the D-T-P skills grid.

**Attitude 3: Deliberate Optimism — Continue learning and creating during unemployment**

Bolles tells the story of Mary, a woman with MS who transformed her life by studying art history during unemployment — not instead of job-hunting, but alongside it. The learning served dual purposes: it maintained self-worth AND created unexpected networking opportunities. "Your time should have two goals: job-hunting and learning, not just one."

### Mary's Story

Bolles tells the story of a woman named Mary with multiple sclerosis who had been told by multiple doctors that nothing could be done. When Bolles asked her "What is multiple sclerosis?" she said "I don't know" — and he asked her "Why don't you become the world's expert on MS?" She did. Within 6 weeks she improved dramatically — not from medical treatment, but from the psychological transformation of becoming an active agent rather than a passive patient. This is Bolles' master metaphor for unemployment: you cannot control whether you're laid off, but you CAN control whether you use the time to become someone more knowledgeable, more skilled, more self-aware.

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** The three attitudes are directly extracted from Bolles' Chapter 3. However, the Birkman/Enneagram mapping is inferred — Bolles doesn't reference Birkman or Enneagram in his book. The alignment patterns are structurally consistent but not empirically validated.

## Stakes

This claim is important because it addresses the emotional layer that the existing project underrepresents. The OSKG has 46 claims covering aptitude, competency, and career fit — but only limited coverage of the psychological resilience needed to execute on those recommendations. Bolles' three attitudes fill this gap: they describe HOW Dan maintains forward momentum during the job-hunting process, not just WHAT he's hunting for.

The Mary story is particularly significant: it demonstrates that unemployment can be a TRANSFORMATIVE period rather than merely a LOST one. For a Type 4 personality (driven by meaning, authenticity, and creative self-expression), this reframing is essential.

## Assessment

The three attitudes form a psychological framework that the Birkman profile describes structurally (Needs, Stress patterns) but doesn't prescribe behaviorally. Bolles provides the behavioral prescription. For Dan specifically, the "at least two alternatives" principle is the most immediately actionable: define himself both as "Systems Administrator / Security Operations" (title-based) AND as "a person who analyzes, secures, and automates IT infrastructure" (skills-based), hunt using both informational interviewing AND targeted applications, target both public-sector AND private-sector roles. This directly counters the Yellow Stress risk of fixating on a single path.
