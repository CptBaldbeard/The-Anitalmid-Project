# anitalmid-ocean-1: Dan maps to O-high, C-high, E-low Big Five profile

**Source:** [[Frameworks/Birkman-to-Framework Crosswalk]] — Section: Crosswalk 4 — Big Five (OCEAN)

## The Claim

Dan Bechtel's Birkman profile maps to the following Big Five (OCEAN) trait profile:
**Very High Openness to Experience**, **Very High Conscientiousness**, **Low
Extraversion**, **Medium-Low Agreeableness**, **Medium-Low Neuroticism**. This is
a classic "creative systematizer" profile — someone who generates novel ideas
(High O) and executes them with discipline (High C), working independently (Low E)
with a task-focused orientation (Medium-Low A) and generally stable emotions
(Medium-Low N).

## Evidence

### Openness — Very High
- Blue Interests: innovation, planning, future-orientation, abstraction, theory
- Artistic interest 94%, Musical 97%, Literary 92% — all O-domain activities
- Scientific interest 92% — intellectual curiosity facet of O
- Ambiguity handling strength (#11) — comfort with complexity, an O marker
- Blue Usual Behavior: creative, insightful

### Conscientiousness — Very High
- Yellow Needs: organized, systematic, consistent rules — C-domain environment
- Structured thinking strength (#7) — core C behavior: order, method
- Reflective efficiency strength (#9) — C self-discipline
- Stress: over-insistent on rules — exaggerated C under pressure
- Technical interest 82% — precision-oriented, C-aligned

### Extraversion — Low
- Blue Usual Behavior: reflective, selectively sociable, thoughtful
- Yellow Needs: don't interrupt, time alone or small groups
- Low Persuasive (32%), Low Social Service (21%)
- Stress: withdrawn, unsociable

### Agreeableness — Medium-Low
- One-on-one sensitivity (#6) and emotional intelligence (#10) suggest MEDIUM
- But: task-orientation over people-orientation, Low Social Service (21%),
  Stress pattern (quietly resistive, critical) pull it LOWER
- Balanced inference: Medium-Low

### Neuroticism — Medium-Low
- Generally calm and reflective Usual Behavior
- Stress pattern is SPECIFIC (triggered by chaos, interruption, over-scheduling)
  rather than GENERAL (pervasive anxiety)
- When Yellow Needs are met: emotionally stable. When violated: stress activates.
- Balanced inference: Medium-Low (situationally higher)

## Confidence

**Rating:** medium
**Rationale:** Inferred from Birkman data, not from direct Big Five assessment. O and C inferences have very-high confidence from multiple converging Birkman signals. E inference is very-high. A and N are the most inferential — agreeableness involves interpersonal behavior not directly measured by Birkman, and Neuroticism requires clinical-style assessment of emotional volatility.

## Stakes

Big Five is the academic gold standard for personality assessment. This inference
gives the Anitalmid career recommendations their strongest scientific backing.
Key implications:

1. **Very High C is the SINGLE BEST predictor of job performance across all
   occupations** (Barrick & Mount, 1991). This predicts Dan will perform well in
   ANY role, not just IT roles. It is the graph's strongest signal for general
   career success.

2. **Very High O + Very High C is a rare combination (~7% of population)**.
   Most people who are highly creative (High O) are lower in Conscientiousness,
   and most highly organized people (High C) are lower in Openness. Dan has both.
   This makes him unusually suited for roles requiring INNOVATION WITHIN
   STRUCTURE — architecture, engineering, technical strategy.

3. **Low E + High O + High C profiles gravitate toward technical/scientific
   careers.** This is the profile of researchers, engineers, architects, and
   senior technical individual contributors.

## Assessment

The Big Five inference is the most structurally significant of the five framework
claims because of the Big Five's superior empirical validation. When this claim
supports a role-fit claim, it carries more weight than an equivalent MBTI or
Enneagram support. The Very High C finding is particularly important — it is the
graph's strongest single signal for general career aptitude, independent of
specific role type.
