# Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)

## Overview

The MBTI, based on Carl Jung's theory of psychological types, classifies
individuals into 16 personality types across four dichotomies. Developed by
Isabel Briggs Myers and Katharine Cook Briggs, it is one of the most widely
used personality frameworks in career counseling and organizational psychology.

**Applications in Anitalmid**: For career aptitude matching, MBTI type provides
a cognitive-style fingerprint — how someone processes information, makes
decisions, and organizes their world. This maps directly to work environment
preferences and role suitability.

## The Four Dichotomies

### E/I — Extraversion vs. Introversion

| Preference | Description | Career Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **E — Extraversion** | Energized by external world of people and activity. Thinks by talking. | Prefers collaborative, interactive, fast-paced environments. Thrives in meetings, presentations, networking. |
| **I — Introversion** | Energized by internal world of ideas and reflection. Thinks before speaking. | Prefers quiet, focused work. Excels at deep analysis, writing, solo creation. Needs recharge time after social interaction. |

### S/N — Sensing vs. Intuition

| Preference | Description | Career Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **S — Sensing** | Focuses on concrete, tangible information. Trusts facts, experience, and details. | Excels at hands-on, practical, detail-oriented work. Prefers proven methods over untested theories. |
| **N — Intuition** | Focuses on patterns, possibilities, and the big picture. Trusts insight and theory. | Excels at strategic planning, innovation, systems thinking. Comfortable with ambiguity and future-oriented work. |

### T/F — Thinking vs. Feeling

| Preference | Description | Career Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **T — Thinking** | Decides based on logic, consistency, and objective analysis. Values fairness through uniform standards. | Excels at analytical roles: engineering, systems design, data analysis, security. Values correctness over consensus. |
| **F — Feeling** | Decides based on personal values and impact on people. Values fairness through individual circumstances. | Excels at people-facing roles: counseling, HR, teaching, customer advocacy. Values harmony and interpersonal outcomes. |

### J/P — Judging vs. Perceiving

| Preference | Description | Career Implications |
|---|---|---|
| **J — Judging** | Prefers structure, planning, and closure. Likes to decide and move forward. | Excels at project management, operations, compliance. Thrives with deadlines, schedules, and clear expectations. |
| **P — Perceiving** | Prefers flexibility, adaptability, and keeping options open. Likes to explore before deciding. | Excels at creative work, research, crisis response. Thrives with autonomy and emergent priorities. |

## Career-Relevant Type Dynamics

### NT Types (INTJ, INTP, ENTJ, ENTP) — The Rationals
- Drive: Competence and knowledge
- Best-fit careers: Technology, science, engineering, strategy, research
- Birkman alignment: Blue/Thinker quadrant, high Scientific and Technical interests

### NF Types (INFJ, INFP, ENFJ, ENFP) — The Idealists
- Drive: Identity and meaning
- Best-fit careers: Counseling, education, arts, social services
- Birkman alignment: People-oriented, high Social Service interest

### SJ Types (ISTJ, ISFJ, ESTJ, ESFJ) — The Guardians
- Drive: Security and belonging
- Best-fit careers: Administration, operations, healthcare, law enforcement
- Birkman alignment: Yellow/Analyzer quadrant, structured and systematic

### SP Types (ISTP, ISFP, ESTP, ESFP) — The Artisans
- Drive: Impact and action
- Best-fit careers: Trades, emergency services, performance, sales
- Birkman alignment: Red/Doer quadrant, hands-on and practical

## Limitations for Career Matching

1. **Type vs. trait**: MBTI categorizes into types (you are E or I) rather than
   measuring on a continuum. Modern research favors trait-based models (Big Five)
   for prediction.
2. **Test-retest reliability**: Up to 50% of people receive a different type on
   retest within a few weeks.
3. **Non-clinical**: MBTI was designed for normal populations, not clinical
   assessment. It does not measure pathology or dysfunction.
4. **Best used for**: Self-awareness, communication style, team dynamics, and
   career EXPLORATION — not definitive career PRESCRIPTION.

## Source and Authority

- **Original research**: Isabel Briggs Myers and Katharine Cook Briggs (1940s-1960s)
- **Based on**: Carl Jung's "Psychological Types" (1921)
- **Current publisher**: The Myers-Briggs Company (formerly CPP, Inc.)
- **Peer-reviewed status**: Moderate — widely used in organizational settings
  but subject to academic criticism for typological approach
- **Apt AI integration**: Apt uses MBTI as one of several frameworks triangulated
  with AI/ML analysis of user responses
