# Folklorist / Mythologist

## Role Overview

Folklorists study the traditional beliefs, myths, legends, customs, and oral
traditions of specific cultures. They analyze how stories encode cultural values,
how myths explain natural and social phenomena, how legends transmit historical
memory, and how folk beliefs reflect a people's environment and lived experience.

This is the specific disciplinary home of Dan's stated interest in *"mythology and
legends"* — folkloristics is the academic study of exactly these materials: why
people told the stories they told, what those stories meant to them, and how the
stories shaped and were shaped by their culture.

## Why This Matches Dan's Profile

Folklore studies operates at the intersection of Dan's three strongest Birkman
interests:

- **Artistic 94%** — myths and legends are narrative art, symbolic expression,
  creative world-building. Studying them requires aesthetic and symbolic intelligence.
- **Literary 92%** — folklorists work primarily with texts (transcribed oral
  traditions, written myth cycles, comparative tale collections). Analysis is
  fundamentally literary.
- **Scientific 92%** — folklore methodology includes the historic-geographic method,
  tale-type indexing (Aarne-Thompson-Uther), structural analysis (Propp's
  morphology), and comparative frameworks. It is systematic research, not casual reading.

The INTJ cognitive style is particularly well-suited: folklore rewards pattern
recognition across cultures (Ni), systematic classification (Te), and comfort
with symbolic/archetypal thinking.

### Sub-fields most aligned:
- **Comparative Mythology** — cross-cultural analysis of myth structures
- **Legend Studies** — how historical memory becomes legendary narrative
- **Folk Belief / Vernacular Religion** — lived belief systems vs. institutional religion
- **Oral Tradition Studies** — how stories change across tellings and generations

### Gaps
- No folklore, mythology, or comparative literature degree
- No language training in relevant source languages (Latin, Greek, Old Norse, etc.)
- No fieldwork experience in oral tradition collection
- No academic publication record
- Extremely niche academic job market (few dedicated folklore departments)

### Entry Path
- **Academic**: MA in Folklore (Indiana University, UC Berkeley, Memorial University of Newfoundland) → PhD
- **Adjacent**: MA in Comparative Literature or Religious Studies with folklore focus
- **Independent**: Public scholar — books, podcasts, YouTube on mythology (Joseph Campbell model, lower barrier)
