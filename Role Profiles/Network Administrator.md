# Network Administrator — Role Profile

## Overview

A Network Administrator designs, implements, and maintains an organization's data
communication infrastructure — the switches, routers, firewalls, and wireless
systems that keep everything connected. This role sits at the intersection of
infrastructure reliability and security, ensuring that data flows securely and
efficiently across LAN, WAN, and cloud environments.

> **Career Aptitude Match**: Network administration rewards systematic,
> methodical thinkers who enjoy deep troubleshooting. The role maps strongly to
> [[analyzer - yellow]] strengths in structured problem-solving and
> [[thinker - blue]] capacity for architectural planning. Dan's
> [[Career History/Telecommunications_Coordinator]] and
> [[Career History/Information_Systems_Specialist]] roles provide direct
> networking-adjacent experience with VoIP migration, MFA deployment, and
> endpoint management.

## Why This Role Matters

Every organization — from hospitals to school districts to Fortune 500
enterprises — depends on network infrastructure. Downtime costs money and
reputation. As organizations adopt hybrid cloud, SD-WAN, and zero-trust
security models, the network administrator's role has evolved from CLI-only
switch jockeys to automation-savvy infrastructure engineers who bridge
on-premises and cloud networking. BLS projects steady demand through 2033, with
network automation skills commanding salary premiums.
