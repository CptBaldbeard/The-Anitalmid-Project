# IT Project Manager — Role Profile

## Overview

An IT Project Manager plans, executes, and closes technology projects — from
software development and cloud migrations to infrastructure refreshes and
security compliance initiatives. This role sits at the intersection of technical
understanding and organizational leadership, translating business requirements
into structured project plans and shepherding cross-functional teams through
delivery.

Unlike general project management, IT PMs need enough technical depth to
understand what their teams are building, estimate effort realistically, and
spot technical risks before they become crises. The best IT PMs are bilingual:
fluent in both engineering and executive communication.

> **Career Aptitude Note**: This role leverages Dan's Blue (creative planning,
> abstract thinking) and Yellow (systematic, organized) Birkman archetypes, but
> requires awareness that Persuasive interest (32%) and Administrative interest
> (20%) are lower than typical PM profiles. The role may work best when
> positioned as a technical PM — managing IT projects where depth of
> understanding compensates for lower natural inclination toward persuasion and
> administration.
> See [[Dan Bechtel Birkman Profile]] for full personality analysis.

## Why This Role Matters

Every major IT initiative — ERP implementations, cloud migrations, security
compliance programs, data center consolidations — requires a PM to keep it on
track. PMI estimates that organizations waste 11.4% of investment due to poor
project performance. Skilled IT PMs recover that value through disciplined
execution. The role is recession-resilient: when budgets tighten, organizations
need PMs even more to ensure remaining investments deliver.

## Day in the Life

See [[#day_in_the_life]] for a typical daily schedule. The rhythm of IT project
management is cyclical — sprint planning and daily standups (Agile), monthly
steering committee reports (governance), quarterly budget reviews (financial),
and annual portfolio planning (strategic). Unlike technical roles, PMs rarely
have on-call rotations — the work is primarily business-hours with predictable
cycles, which aligns well with Dan's Yellow Needs for systematic environment.

### Birkman Alignment Summary

investigating, one-on-one sensitivity — all directly applicable to project
management.

**Caution areas**: Low Persuasive (32%) requires deliberate skill-building for
stakeholder influence; Low Administrative (20%) means the administrative
paperwork will be a discipline rather than a joy. The Yellow stress response
(becoming rigid on rules) could paradoxically be adaptive in PM — structured
process is a strength when projects get chaotic — but must be balanced with
flexibility.

**Net assessment**: IT Project Manager is a **moderate-high fit** for Dan's
profile. The technical depth from his systems background compensates for lower
natural persuasion. Best suited as a *second career move* after deepening
technical architecture skills in a Systems Administrator or Architect role,
